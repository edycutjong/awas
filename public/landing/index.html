Looks it up

Checks every quote

Nemotron (call 2, nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b) proposes evidence lines over the pages Tavily returned. Code accepts a quote only if it has at least 6 words and is a verbatim substring of one chunk fetched in this run. A line that fails is dropped on screen, greyed and struck; the claim beside a quote is labelled “Awas’s reading — not verified”.

✓ verified → highlighter, link with a text fragment to the sentence

Decides in code

Questions a judge would ask.

Does Awas ever say an ad is safe?

No. There are three verdicts — STOP, VERIFY FIRST and NO RED FLAGS FOUND — and the last one says, on screen, that it is not a guarantee. There is no green anywhere in the product and no score.

What if the model invents a quote?

The gate drops it, visibly. A quote is shown only if it has at least 6 words and appears verbatim in one chunk of a page Tavily returned in that run. A flipped clause or a paraphrase fails — you can try it in the lab above.

Who decides STOP — the model?

Code. verdict.ts returns STOP for a NEVER rule with a recorded official source, or for a verified line that names the ad’s own company together with a harm term and no denial term. The model chooses where to look and proposes lines; it cannot set the verdict.

Does it accuse companies?

Awas names a company only inside a verified quote or the attribution “Dilaporkan oleh <sumber>, bukan tuduhan kami” (reported by the source, not our accusation). Its own sentences never name the company; recordings of real runs mask company and person names.

How accurate is it?

Not measured yet. The benchmark protocol and the stage-T scoring rules were committed before any row was collected or any model ran; the results go to DEMO.md when they exist, and only then onto this page.

Can I try it without an account?

Yes — no login and no daily cap. Live checks are limited to 60 per hour from one connection, and the judge code in the Devpost testing notes lifts that limit.
